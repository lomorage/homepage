A new phone is exciting — until you realize years of photos live on the old one. Switching between **iPhone and Android** makes it trickier, because each side's built-in tools are designed to keep you in their ecosystem. Here's a safe way to switch without leaving a single memory behind.

<!--more-->

## The golden rule: back up first, transfer second

Transfer apps (Apple's Move to iOS, manufacturers' switch tools) are useful, but they're a one-shot process. If a video is skipped or the transfer stalls, you won't know until the old phone is already wiped. So:

1. **Back up everything** from the old phone to a place you control.
2. **Verify** the backup.
3. **Transfer** to the new phone however you like.
4. **Only then** reset the old phone.

## Step 1: Back up the old phone at home

[Lomorage](/) works on both iPhone and Android and backs up originals over Wi-Fi to your Windows PC, Mac or NAS:

- Install the free desktop app from the [download section](/#download).
- Install LomoMobile on the **old** phone, scan the setup code, create your account.
- Let the first backup finish with the app open and the phone charging.

Detailed guides: [iPhone](/blog/2026/11/24/backup-iphone-photos-without-icloud/) · [Android](/blog/2026/11/24/backup-android-photos-to-computer/).

On iPhone, if "Optimize iPhone Storage" is on, download originals from iCloud first — otherwise you may only back up previews.

## Step 2: Verify

On the computer, open a sample of photos and videos — oldest, newest, a long video, a Live Photo. Roughly compare the count with the old phone's library.

## Step 3: Set up the new phone

Install LomoMobile on the new phone and sign in to **the same account**. Your backed-up library is there to browse and search, whether the new phone is an iPhone or an Android. New photos from the new phone back up to the same place.

On iOS you can also save backed-up photos to the phone locally when you want them on the device.

## Step 4: Reset the old phone

With a verified backup at home, you can reset the old phone with confidence — or turn an old Android tablet into a [photo frame](/blog/2021/08/26/zero-cost-android-frame/).

## Cross-platform gotchas

- **HEIC:** iPhone photos are often HEIC. Recent Android phones can open them; keep the originals regardless ([HEIC vs JPG](/blog/2026/11/25/heic-vs-jpg/)).
- **Live Photos:** Android galleries may show only the still image. The original backup keeps the whole Live Photo.
- **Messaging app media:** photos inside chat apps aren't always in the camera roll. On Android, choose those folders to back up; for others, use the chat app's own export.
