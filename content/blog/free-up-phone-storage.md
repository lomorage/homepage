"Storage Almost Full" always seems to appear right before the moment you want to film. The fastest fix — deleting photos — is also the riskiest. Here is a safe order of operations to reclaim space on iPhone or Android **without losing a single memory**.

<!--more-->

## Step 1: See what's actually using space

- **iPhone:** Settings → General → iPhone Storage.
- **Android:** Settings → Storage (names vary by manufacturer).

In most family phones, **Photos / Gallery is the largest item**, and within it **videos** dominate. One minute of 4K video can take hundreds of megabytes, more than a hundred photos.

## Step 2: Back up originals before deleting anything

This is the step people skip. Before you remove anything, make sure a full-quality copy exists somewhere else:

- Sharing through a messaging app is **not** a backup — images and videos are usually compressed.
- A cloud service works, but on the free tier it often fills up quickly.
- A home backup keeps originals on a drive you own. With [Lomorage](/), your phone backs up photos, videos and Live Photos over Wi-Fi to your Windows PC, Mac or NAS. See [how to back up iPhone photos without iCloud](/blog/2026/11/24/backup-iphone-photos-without-icloud/) or [Android photos to a computer](/blog/2026/11/24/backup-android-photos-to-computer/).

## Step 3: Verify the backup

Open a handful of photos and **especially videos** from the backup location — the newest, the oldest, and a Live Photo. Check that the count roughly matches your phone's library. Two minutes here prevents the worst kind of regret.

## Step 4: Clear the biggest files first

Deleting a few large videos frees more space than hundreds of photos. In LomoMobile, **Free Up Space** removes large local files that are already backed up, so you're not guessing which ones are safe.

## Step 5: Tidy duplicates and near-identical shots

Burst shots and "take ten to get one" photos add up. Review duplicate and similar photos and keep the best one. Lomorage helps you review duplicate and similar photos so you choose what to keep — nothing is removed without your decision.

## Step 6: Other quick wins

- **Recently Deleted:** on iPhone and many Android phones, deleted photos stay for about 30 days. Empty it only after you've verified your backup.
- **Messaging app media:** chat apps often store large amounts of forwarded videos and images. Clean them up inside the app's storage settings.
- **Offload unused apps:** iPhone can offload apps you rarely use while keeping their data.

## A note on "optimized" storage

iCloud's "Optimize iPhone Storage" and similar options keep small previews on the phone and full originals in the cloud. That saves space, but it also means your phone doesn't hold a complete copy — make sure your backup tool can reach the originals before relying on it.

## The safe-space checklist

1. Find the biggest category.
2. Back up originals.
3. Verify the backup opens correctly.
4. Remove large backed-up files.
5. Review duplicates and similar photos.
6. Keep a second independent copy of irreplaceable photos ([the 3-2-1 rule](/blog/2026/11/24/321-backup-family-photos/)).
