The device you choose stores the original photos and should have enough free space for your growing library.

## Familiar computers

**[Windows](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-win/)** and **[macOS](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-osx/)** are the simplest choices. They are a good fit when you want to try Lomorage with hardware you already know.

## Always-on home storage

Use an existing **[Synology NAS](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-synology/)**, **[Unraid](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-unraid/)** or **[Docker](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-docker/)** server if you are comfortable maintaining it.

## Linux and small home servers

The Lomorage backend is published for Linux `amd64`, `arm64`, and `armhf`, covering common x86_64 computers, 64-bit ARM devices, and 32-bit ARM devices. Guides are available for **[Raspberry Pi](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-raspbian/)**, **[Armbian](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-armbian/)**, **[Ubuntu](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-ubuntu/)**, **[Fedora](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-fedora/)**, **[Rocky Linux 9](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-rocky9/)**, **[openSUSE Leap](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-opensuse/)** and **[OpenWrt](/docs/Installation/lomorage-service/installation-openwrt/)**.

Your originals remain ordinary files on disk. Whichever setup you choose, keep another independent copy of irreplaceable photos.
